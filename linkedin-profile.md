## About

I have spent my career learning how to compete, build relationships, recognize opportunity and bring the right people together around it.

Today, I do that at STRATUM Electric, where I focus on business development at the intersection of electrical infrastructure, commercial construction, data centers, AI infrastructure and mission-critical power.

Looking back, the path makes more sense than it probably did while I was living it.

I began my professional career in one of the most competitive environments imaginable: professional golf. At the University of Southern California, I competed at the highest level of collegiate golf and earned All-American recognition. I turned professional in 1998 and went on to compete on the PGA TOUR and what is now the Korn Ferry Tour, earning three tour victories and playing 80 PGA TOUR events.

Golf taught me much more than how to play. It taught me how to prepare when nobody is watching, read conditions before making a decision, stay focused when circumstances change, build relationships across a wide range of people, and perform when the outcome matters.

Most importantly, it taught me that success is rarely about one moment. It is about consistently putting yourself in position to take advantage of the next opportunity.

That philosophy followed me into business.

I moved into commercial real estate, where I learned how owners, developers, tenants, brokers and capital interact to create transactions. I then expanded into financial services and title-related business development, gaining another perspective on the trust, risk and relationships that sit underneath commercial real estate.

In 2017, I moved deeper into construction with Hathaway Dinwiddie and later continued that work with W. L. Butler.

That chapter changed my perspective again.

I was no longer simply looking at transactions. I was getting closer to the people responsible for turning major opportunities into physical projects—owners, developers, general contractors, consultants and project stakeholders.

I began to understand how projects really develop: often long before a shovel hits the ground, and sometimes long before the broader market even knows the opportunity exists.

That experience ultimately led me to STRATUM Electric.

Today, I am particularly interested in the projects where technology, real estate, construction and power infrastructure converge: data centers, AI infrastructure, mission-critical facilities, large-scale electrical systems and complex commercial developments.

These projects require more than technical capability.

They require timing. They require trust. They require an understanding of who influences a project, who makes decisions, where an opportunity is actually coming from, and how to get the right people connected early enough to create value.

That is where I add value.

I build relationships before they are needed. I look for opportunities before they become obvious. I connect owners, developers, contractors, technology companies and other stakeholders when there is a reason for them to know each other.

And I work to turn those relationships into opportunities that can become projects—and projects into long-term partnerships.

In many ways, the different chapters of my career were preparing me for exactly this.

Competitive golf taught me how to perform.

Real estate taught me how deals come together.

Financial services taught me the importance of trust and transaction infrastructure.

Construction taught me how projects actually get built.

And STRATUM brings those experiences together around one of the defining infrastructure challenges of the next decade:

**building the electrical infrastructure that powers what comes next.**
